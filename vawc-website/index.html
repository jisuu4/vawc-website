Know the issue

What is VAWC?

Under Republic Act No. 9262, VAWC is any act or series of acts committed by a person against a woman who is his wife, former wife, or with whom he has or had a sexual or dating relationship, or with whom he has a common child — or against her child — resulting in physical, sexual, psychological harm or suffering, or economic abuse. Know the law

Laws that protect women and children

The Philippines has strong laws against VAWC. Knowing them is the first step to using them.

  • VAWC is a public crime — a complaint may be filed by any citizen with personal knowledge of the incident, not only the victim (Sec. 25).
  • Covers dating relationships — not just marriage. Boyfriends, ex-boyfriends, live-in partners and fathers of the woman's child are covered (Sec. 3).
  • Penalties include imprisonment, a fine of ₱100,000 to ₱300,000, and mandatory psychological counseling or psychiatric treatment (Sec. 6).
  • Battered Woman Syndrome is recognized as a valid defense (Sec. 26).
  • Victims are entitled to up to 10 days of paid leave in addition to other leave benefits (Sec. 43).

Protection Orders under RA 9262

A protection order stops the abuser from further harming, contacting, or coming near the victim.

Barangay Protection Order

Issued by: the Punong Barangay (or a Barangay Kagawad if the Punong Barangay is unavailable)

Valid for: 15 days

The fastest form of protection. Orders the abuser to stop committing or threatening physical harm. Go to your Barangay VAW Desk.

Temporary Protection Order

Issued by: the court (Family Court or Regional Trial Court)

Valid for: 30 days, extendable

Can include orders to stay away from the victim, leave the shared home, give temporary custody of children, and provide financial support.

Permanent Protection Order

Issued by: the court, after a hearing

Valid: until revoked by the court

Long-term protection that can include all the reliefs of a TPO, made permanent.

Source: RA 9262, Secs. 8, 14–16 [4]

If someone tells you they are being abused

  1. Listen and believe. Don't blame, judge, or pressure them.
  2. Tell them it's not their fault. Remind them that help exists.
  3. Share the hotlines and offer to go with them to the Barangay VAW Desk.
  4. Respect their pace — but if a child or anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.
